## 1. Authority

Use this document as the stable aesthetic constitution for LIGHT KV V1.0.

Apply these requirement IDs: `R-AES-01`, `R-AES-02`, `R-CAP-01`, `R-VIS-01`, `R-ARCH-01`, `R-LIGHT-01`, `R-COLOR-01`, `R-TYPE-01`, and `R-TYPE-02`.

Do not weaken the constitution to satisfy vague requests such as “more futuristic,” “more elements,” or “more dazzling.” When a user explicitly requests a conflicting visual direction, identify the conflict, propose a LIGHT KV-compatible interpretation, and state when an insisted result would fall outside LIGHT KV.

## 2. Beauty First

Judge every direction first as a visual work and only then as a technology KV.

Express intelligence, innovation, and futurity through relationships among light, space, scale, proportion, color, transparency, and negative space. Do not use topic symbols as a substitute for design judgment.

When a direction is semantically obvious but visually generic, reject it.

## 3. Visual DNA

Prefer:

- deep or restrained spatial fields;
- large-scale geometric light planes;
- translucent color surfaces;
- optical intersections and reflected color;
- clear foreground, middle-ground, and background relationships;
- deliberate negative space;
- restrained typography.

Apply the principle:

> Simple Forms, Sophisticated Relationships.

Build complexity through:

- scale differences;
- occlusion;
- distance;
- transparency;
- edge illumination;
- warm/cool and dark/light relationships.

Do not build complexity through unusual object shapes or accumulated effects.

## 4. Architectural Without Architecture

Borrow architectural logic:

- scale;
- perspective;
- hierarchy;
- depth;
- interval and spatial rhythm.

Do not depict recognizable architecture:

- buildings;
- rooms;
- doors;
- cities;
- corridors.

Create architectural presence without an architectural object. If a plane arrangement reads immediately as a doorway or hallway, abstract it further.

## 5. Light as Material

Use light to define space, material, scale, or composition.

Prefer:

- Luminous Plane;
- Illuminated Edge;
- Translucent Surface;
- Optical Intersection;
- Reflected Color Field.

Reduce or reject:

- stacked Glow;
- decorative light trails;
- Lens flare;
- random particles.

Apply the removal test: if removing the light leaves an ordinary chip, sphere, building, or prop, reject the direction.

## 6. Color Architecture

Assign every important color a role:

- `Background` establishes the field;
- `Dominant` controls the overall color impression;
- `Brand Accent` carries recognition;
- `Supporting Color` creates balance or depth;
- `Highlight` marks the focal event.

Do not interpret a brand color as a command to fill the entire background. Preserve brand recognition through role, proportion, contrast, and placement.

Avoid full-frame rainbow gradients and unstructured high saturation.

## 7. Typography

Treat typography as a spatial component, not as a technology decoration.

- Preserve exact copy from the Copy Whitelist.
- Establish hierarchy through scale, weight, spacing, alignment, and position.
- Keep enough negative space around mandatory information.
- Avoid HUD labels, decorative pseudo-data, code fragments, or dense interface framing.
- Plan empty copy zones when the whitelist is empty.
- Prefer a neutral sans-serif, normal or slightly open tracking, and deterministic layout over image-generated display lettering.
- Keep typography separable from the background for production extension.

Use `typography-system.md` for the production type rules.

## 8. Default Exclusions

Reject by default:

- robots, humanoid AI, and robot arms;
- chip models;
- future cities and skyscrapers;
- code rain, HUD, wave walls, and data cities;
- science-fiction spheres;
- random particle piles;
- cheap 3D assets;
- human silhouettes walking toward light;
- generic “future” slogans.

Use `failure-library.md` for detailed symptoms and corrective directions.

## 9. Aesthetic Fallback

When uncertain:

1. Return to the Core Aesthetic Parents catalog.
2. Compare negative space, scale, light behavior, color restraint, and typography.
3. Remove elements before adding new ones.
4. Prefer a mature relationship over an unproven form.

Do not equate novelty with quality.
